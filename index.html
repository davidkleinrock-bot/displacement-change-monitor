About the data

What each measure counts

  • Refugees under UNHCR's mandate: people recognized as refugees or in refugee-like situations. Palestine refugees under UNRWA's mandate are not included.
  • Asylum-seekers: people whose asylum claims were still pending at year-end.
  • All needing international protection: refugees, asylum-seekers and "other people in need of international protection," a group UNHCR reports from 2018 onward.
  • Internally displaced (IDMC): people displaced by conflict and violence inside their own country, as estimated by the Internal Displacement Monitoring Centre.

How change is calculated

Every figure is a count on 31 December. Change is the end-year count minus the start-year count. Percentage change is shown only when the start figure is above zero. Ranking by % leaves out countries that started under 1,000 people, so tiny numbers don't top the list.

If either year has no figure, the country is marked unavailable and is not ranked. "Country of origin" counts people by where they fled from. "Host country" counts them by where they live now.

What the arrows show

Each arrow runs from a country of origin to a host country. In "Change" mode, its width is how much that group grew or shrank over the period: red for growth, blue for decline. In "Total at end" mode, its width is the number of people at the end of the period.

Arrows compare year-end counts. They are not counts of journeys. A shrinking corridor can mean returns, moves elsewhere, naturalization or a revised count. Arrows start and end at a reference point inside each country. People of unknown origin or who are stateless have no point on the map, so they appear in lists only.

Checks on the numbers

Each origin-to-host figure is added up and compared with UNHCR's own published totals.

The ranking compares the same two dates for every country. It flags situations worth a closer look. It is not a full assessment of humanitarian conditions.

Read before relying on a ranking

Large swings can come from how a government counts people, not only from new displacement or returns. In UNHCR's 2025 figures, refugees hosted in Iran fell from 3.49 million to 0.77 million, and people of unknown origin needing international protection rose by about 1.5 million. Check UNHCR's notes before reading a swing like that as movement on the ground.

UNHCR publishes year-end figures once a year, months after the year closes. The newest comparison is therefore at least several months old.

News headlines

Headlines come from GDELT, which tracks news coverage worldwide and tags each article with the countries it covers. Each day the update reads every English-language article GDELT logged in the past 24 hours and keeps those whose headline mentions refugees, displacement, asylum, evacuations, migrants or deportations. Headlines stay for 14 days. They are matched automatically and not checked by this tool, so some may be off-topic.

Updates

On the project's GitHub Pages site, a scheduled job downloads all figures and headlines again every day and re-runs the checks before publishing. If a check fails, the previous data stays up. When UNHCR adds a new year, the default comparison moves to it.
